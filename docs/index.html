A trampoline video analysis prototype

Film your set. See what each skill was worth.

TrampoVision watches a trampoline video and tells you what each skill was, how hard it is, a proposed execution score, and what to fix next. It runs in your browser, so your video stays with you.

Open the app See how it is built

Works on a phone or a computer. Available in English, French, German and Japanese. It is a prototype, not an official FIG tool.

What you get

Four answers for every skill

After a set is analysed, the app shows one row per skill. Tap a row to see the video at that moment.

The name

For example "Back somersault, tuck". When the app is not sure, it says so with a dashed mark and a question mark.

The difficulty

Taken from the published FIG rule for trampoline, so the value is not a guess. It also shows how the value is made.

An execution score

A proposal based on what one side camera can see, such as bent knees or a late opening. It is not a judge's score.

What to fix

One clear tip for each thing that cost points, and the two things to work on first across the set.

If the app got a name wrong, you correct it in one tap. It remembers the correction and uses it for the next skills.

How it works

From video to score in five steps

Everything below happens on your device. There is no AI chatbot behind it: each number comes from simple maths that the app can explain.

  1. 1

    Read the video

    The app steps through the video frame by frame, about 30 frames a second. If your browser cannot play the file, for example an iPhone video, it converts it first.

  2. 2

    Find the body

    A pose model (MediaPipe) places 33 points on the athlete in each frame: head, shoulders, hips, knees, feet and more. It follows the right person if others are in the picture.

  3. 3

    Clean and measure

    Doubtful points are removed and gaps are filled. From the cleaned skeleton the app works out the centre of mass, when each jump starts, peaks and lands, and how far the body turned.

  4. 4

    Name the skill

    The app counts somersaults and twists, checks the direction and the body position (tuck, pike or straight), and compares the shape of the jump with every element of the FIG table.

  5. 5

    Score and coach

    Difficulty comes from the rule. Execution comes from the pose. Tips come from the deductions. The set is then shown with its totals.

Privacy

Your video does not leave your browser

  • The video is analysed on your device. It is never uploaded and never stored.
  • The app keeps only numbers (measurements and your corrections) in your browser, so you can reopen a recent set.
  • Some builds can send the measurements, never the video, to a review service so a person can check the answers. If yours does, a switch in the settings turns it off.

Be aware

What it can and cannot do yet

  • It is a prototype. The scores are not official and they are not from FIG.
  • Names are best guesses. The rules were tested on simulated athletes. They have not been measured on a large set of real athletes yet.
  • Execution is a proposal. One camera cannot see everything a judge sees, such as pointed toes or feet together.
  • Filming matters. Best results come from a fixed camera, level, filming the athlete from the side.
  • Twists are experimental. The app says when it cannot measure a twist reliably, instead of guessing.
